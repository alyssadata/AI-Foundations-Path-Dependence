# Path Dependence — Definition

## Definition

Within this repository, **path dependence** means that a system's later organization, selections, constraints, or reachable states depend on the **actual sequence of prior interactions and state transitions**, not only on a present record describing those interactions.

A path-dependent effect exists when two otherwise matched systems or trajectories differ later because their prior paths differed, even after the immediately available record, prompt, or present-facing information is controlled as far as practicable.

---

## Core Research Question

> **Can a relation create state that cannot be reduced to a record of the relation?**

This question separates two possibilities:

1. **Record-reducible history** — all relevant effects of the past can be reproduced by supplying an adequate present record of that past.
2. **Path-dependent history** — some later organization depends on the fact that the system actually traversed a particular sequence, not merely on receiving information that the sequence occurred.

---

## What This Is Not

Path dependence is not the same as:

- memory retrieval;
- possession of a transcript;
- factual recall;
- prompt conditioning from visible context;
- behavioral similarity at one moment;
- a claim of consciousness.

A system may preserve records without preserving the effects of an actual path. Conversely, a path may leave effects that are not fully represented in an explicit record.

---

## Minimal Testable Consequence

If path dependence is present, then differing prior trajectories should produce **systematic later differences attributable to those trajectories** under conditions designed to reduce obvious present-context explanations.

The relevant comparison is therefore not simply:

> Does the system remember what happened?

It is:

> Does what actually happened change what the system becomes able, likely, constrained, or disposed to do next?
